You wanted a film. Now you’re forty minutes into scrolling past faces with their mouths open, and the only thing frightening you is tomorrow’s alarm.

The problem with searching for the best horror movies on Netflix is that “horror” covers wildly different evenings. One person wants a demon in the corridor. Another wants something disgusting enough to interrupt dinner. Someone else wants a good film that happens to make being alive feel slightly less comfortable.

**Our best overall pick is His House. For supernatural dread, choose Incantation. For a bloody, comparatively playful group watch, choose The Babysitter.** The six films below count down from the least to the most disturbing in our editorial ranking. Quality gets its own score: a film does not become better simply because it makes you feel worse.

<h2 id="the-babysitter">6. The Babysitter: blood on the carpet, tongue in cheek</h2>

**2017 · 85 minutes · English · Netflix UK certificate: 15**

A boy stays awake and discovers his babysitter’s evening involves a satanic cult. The basic betrayal is excellent horror machinery: the person hired to keep you safe is precisely the person you should avoid. Netflix classifies this as both horror and comedy. [Netflix UK listing](https://www.netflix.com/gb/title/80164456).

The reason to choose it is tonal permission. Its exaggerated violence and comic energy make room for laughter rather than demanding reverent silence. This is the least emotionally oppressive selection here, which emphatically does not mean the least bloody. Put it on when the group wants horror but nobody wants an existential debrief afterwards.

**Disturbance: 2/5. Content notes:** bloody violence, sexual references and a child in danger. **Skip it if:** jokey cruelty irritates you more than it entertains you. [Credits and runtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Babysitter_(2017_film)).

<h2 id="his-house">5. His House: the best film here, and the hardest one to shrug off</h2>

**2020 · 93 minutes · English and Dinka · Netflix UK certificate: 15**

A couple fleeing South Sudan are placed in an English home where something sinister is waiting. Wunmi Mosaku and Ṣọpẹ Dìrísù play the couple; Remi Weekes directs. [Netflix UK listing](https://www.netflix.com/gb/title/81231197) · [Credits and runtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_House).

A haunted house usually invites the obvious question: why don’t they leave? Here, the search for safety makes that question painfully inadequate. Home is shelter, an obligation and an unfamiliar place whose rules must be learned. The haunting puts pressure on all three.

That is why this gets our highest quality score without taking the top disturbance spot. The fear has a human structure underneath it. A wall can be frightening; the possibility that safety will never feel safe is worse.

<LocoRating score={5} scope="His House (2020)" why="Its haunted-house premise and refugee story give each other force. The setting creates both immediate scares and a deeper argument about belonging, survival and guilt." caveat="Grief and displacement make this a heavy choice for an evening intended as escapism." bestFor="Viewers who want emotionally substantial horror rather than a sequence of shocks." />

**Disturbance: 4/5. Content notes:** war-related trauma, child loss and supernatural threat. **Choose it over Apostle if:** emotional consequence interests you more than physical brutality.

<h2 id="apostle">4. Apostle: a rural retreat with an exceptionally bad complaints procedure</h2>

**2018 · 129 minutes · English · Netflix UK certificate: 18**

In 1905, a man travels to an isolated island to recover his kidnapped sister from a religious cult. Dan Stevens and Michael Sheen star in Gareth Evans’s folk horror film. [Netflix UK listing](https://www.netflix.com/gb/title/80158148) · [Credits and runtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostle_(film)).

The island setting does useful work before anything overtly horrific happens. A closed community controls your movements, your information and the terms on which you are tolerated. The atmosphere is damp, suspicious and increasingly hostile; the violence gives that hostility a physical expression.

This is the longest commitment on the list. Pick it for a full descent into a rotten little world, rather than a quick succession of scares before bed.

**Disturbance: 4/5. Content notes:** torture, graphic injury and religious coercion. **Skip it if:** prolonged physical suffering is your limit. Its appeal is not dependent on being surprised by that warning.

<h2 id="the-platform">3. The Platform: probably eat first</h2>

**2019 · 94 minutes · Spanish · Netflix UK certificate: 18**

Food descends through a vertical institution on a platform. Those above take what they want; those below inherit the consequences. Netflix labels this Spanish film a thriller. We include it for its bodily horror and sustained grotesquerie. [Netflix UK listing](https://www.netflix.com/gb/title/81128579).

Galder Gaztelu-Urrutia’s film turns inequality into architecture. You do not need a speech about the distribution of resources when you can watch dinner disappear before it reaches someone who needs it. The premise also sets a nasty trap for the viewer: it is easy to imagine behaving generously before imagining yourself hungry.

Its bluntness is both the attraction and the limitation. This is an argument delivered through stomach-turning circumstances, with very little interest in making the metaphor discreet.

The confined setting gives each decision about food and survival an ugly weight." caveat="The symbolism is forceful rather than subtle, and the ending may frustrate anyone seeking tidy explanations." bestFor="Viewers who like dystopian ideas mixed with confrontational physical horror." />

**Disturbance: 5/5. Content notes:** starvation, cannibalism and graphic violence. **Choose it for:** disgust and moral discomfort, rather than ghosts. [Credits and runtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Platform_(film)).

<h2 id="geralds-game">2. Gerald’s Game: nowhere to go, including inside your own head</h2>

**2017 · 103 minutes · English · Netflix UK certificate: 18**

Jessie is handcuffed to a bed in a remote house when a sexual game with her husband goes catastrophically wrong. Carla Gugino leads Mike Flanagan’s adaptation of Stephen King’s novel. [Netflix UK listing](https://www.netflix.com/gb/title/80128722) · [Credits and runtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald%27s_Game_(film)).

The restriction is the point. Ordinary objects acquire enormous importance when reaching them becomes a survival problem. Meanwhile, physical vulnerability leaves Jessie exposed to memories and fears she cannot simply walk away from.

This earns its high disturbance position through intimacy. Large-scale horror gives you somewhere else to look. A trapped person, a small room and an increasingly desperate set of possibilities offer very little distance. There is also an injury sequence for which “squeamish viewers beware” feels almost administratively polite.

Gugino’s central role gives the predicament a human focus beyond the mechanics of escape." caveat="The closing explanations are less powerful than the immediate predicament, and the trauma material is substantial." bestFor="Viewers who prefer claustrophobic psychological horror to a roaming monster." />

**Disturbance: 5/5. Content notes:** childhood sexual abuse, coercion and graphic injury. **Skip it if:** those subjects will overwhelm the pleasure of the thriller.

<h2 id="incantation">1. Incantation: when watching starts to feel like participating</h2>

**2022 · 111 minutes · Primarily Mandarin · Netflix UK certificate: 15**

Li Ronan broke a religious taboo six years earlier. Now her daughter is threatened by the consequences. Kevin Ko’s Taiwanese film presents its story through found footage and appeals directed at the viewer. [Netflix UK listing](https://www.netflix.com/gb/title/81599888) · [Credits, format and runtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incantation_(film)).

Our top spot comes down to the relationship it builds with the audience. A conventional haunting happens to somebody else. Incantation keeps making the act of looking feel less passive. Symbols, repetition and requests for attention turn the screen from a window into something that seems to want a response.

That does not make its curse real. It makes the filmmaking device effective: the film gives your imagination a small job, then lets that participation become uncomfortable. Avoid plot summaries that explain the mechanism before you watch.

**Disturbance: 5/5. Content notes:** child endangerment, disturbing bodily imagery and ritual violence. **Choose it for:** the feeling that switching off the television has not quite finished the evening.

## Which one should you actually watch tonight?

If you only watch one, make it **His House**. It offers the strongest combination here of a distinctive premise, emotional stakes and a reason for the horror to exist beyond making you jump.

For a **group watch**, The Babysitter allows conversation and laughter without fighting the film’s tone. For a **quiet, concentrated watch**, Incantation benefits from attention to the images and instructions. For **physical horror**, Apostle is the more sprawling descent; Gerald’s Game keeps the pressure painfully close.

If you are undecided between The Platform and His House, ask whether you want the evening’s conversation to be about a system or about people living with the past. Both have ideas; they place the emotional weight differently.

## Questions worth answering before pressing play

### What is the scariest horror movie on Netflix in this shortlist?

Incantation takes our top spot for supernatural dread and the way it implicates the viewer. Gerald’s Game may be more distressing if realistic injury or abuse-related material affects you more than curses. There is no universal scare ranking.

### Which is the best horror movie here, regardless of scariness?

His House earns our only 5/5 quality rating. Its treatment of safety, belonging and guilt gives the haunted-house story a purpose that outlasts the immediate scares. The separate disturbance score explains why it is not number one in the countdown.

### Is there a less frightening option?

The Babysitter has the most playful tone and our lowest disturbance score. It still contains bloody violence and sexual references. “Less frightening” is a comparison within this adult-oriented shortlist, not a recommendation for children.

### Which films are under 100 minutes?

The Babysitter is 85 minutes, His House is 93 and The Platform is 94. Those runtimes make them easier to fit into a late start; their emotional effects are a different scheduling problem.
