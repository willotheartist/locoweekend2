London has hundreds of companies that can build an app. That is not the same thing as having hundreds of companies you should trust with an app that matters to your business.

The useful distinction is not simply iOS versus Android or React Native versus Flutter. It is **what kind of product problem the team is built to solve**.

A founder launching a first paid product needs speed, scope discipline and ownership. A retailer replacing a loyalty app needs data, integrations and long-term optimisation. A regulated financial-services company needs security and governance. A business with an existing platform may need app development to sit inside a wider product-modernisation programme.

This guide compares **eight app development companies serving the London market in 2026** across product thinking, mobile and web capability, engineering approach, scale, public pricing signals and buyer fit.

Some have physical London offices; others are UK teams actively serving London clients. We state that distinction rather than pretending geography is more precise than it is.

## First decide whether you actually need a mobile app

This is the question good app companies should be willing to ask before taking your money.

A responsive web application is often the better first product when users visit occasionally, discover the service through search, or need instant access without installing anything. Native or cross-platform mobile becomes more compelling when usage is frequent, push notifications matter, the product uses device capabilities, users operate on the move, or one-tap home-screen access changes behaviour.

A company that automatically recommends native iOS and Android because “you asked for an app” is not necessarily doing product strategy.

## Native iOS/Android or cross-platform in 2026?

For most buyers, this should not be a belief system.

**React Native and Flutter** can be excellent choices when one product needs to cover both iOS and Android efficiently and does not depend on unusual platform-specific capabilities. thoughtbot explicitly describes React Native as the right balance for many first-version apps, while Stakk and 3 SIDED CUBE both support cross-platform development.

**Native Swift/Kotlin** remains appropriate when performance, specialised device APIs, platform-specific interaction or existing native codebases justify the additional cost and duplicated engineering effort.

A good app company should explain the trade-off in the context of your product rather than selling whichever framework its developers already prefer.

## What should a serious app development proposal contain?

For most non-trivial app projects, expect clarity on:

- target users and core jobs-to-be-done;
- whether mobile is actually the right platform;
- UX research, flows and interface design;
- native versus cross-platform reasoning;
- frontend/mobile development;
- APIs, backend and database work;
- authentication and permissions;
- payments or subscriptions where relevant;
- analytics, monitoring and crash reporting;
- QA across devices;
- App Store / Play Store release process;
- code, infrastructure and account ownership;
- support and iteration after launch.

## FAQ: What is the best app development company in London?

There is no universal best. Apadmi is better aligned with large-scale brand and loyalty apps; thoughtbot with engineering-led product teams; Stakk with funded mobile-first companies; Wall & Fifth with smaller senior-led fixed-price builds; and 3 SIDED CUBE with purpose-led organisations.

## FAQ: How long does it take to build an app?

A focused MVP can often be built in roughly two months. More complex mobile apps commonly take several months, particularly where there are multiple user types, third-party integrations, regulated data, existing-system migration or native development on two platforms.

## FAQ: Should a startup build a web app or mobile app first?

If installation, push notifications, device capabilities or frequent on-the-go use are not central to the product, a responsive web app is often the more efficient first release. A good development company should help make that decision before scope is locked.

## FAQ: Who should own the App Store account and source code?

Ideally the client should control the production accounts, source repository, infrastructure and store listings, even if the development company manages them during delivery. This reduces lock-in and makes future handover materially easier.
