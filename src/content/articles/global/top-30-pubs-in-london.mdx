London is one of the last cities where the pub still feels like civic infrastructure.

A good one can be a dining room, a storm shelter, a first-date filter, a literary prop, a football annex, a lunch break, a bad-idea starter, or the only room in the city where everyone briefly agrees on the same thing: one more.

This is not a list of “best pubs” in the lazy algorithmic sense. It is a list of real rooms with atmosphere, location, memory, and enough point of view to justify the journey.

## What makes a great London pub?

Usually some combination of the following:

- a room with real character
- a strong location or neighbourhood role
- beer that suits the room
- food that does not embarrass the room
- enough history to matter, but not so much it feels preserved under glass

The best pubs in London do not just pour drinks. They shape evenings.

## Historic London institutions

### 1. Ye Olde Cheshire Cheese, Fleet Street

One of the most famous pub rooms in London, and still worth it. It was rebuilt in **1667** after the Great Fire, and the appeal now is the same as ever: dark timber, narrow passages, low ceilings, and a sense that the building has absorbed several centuries of argument. [Venue](https://ye-olde-cheshire-cheese.co.uk/) · [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Ye+Olde+Cheshire+Cheese+145+Fleet+Street+London)

### 2. The George Inn, Southwark

The George earns its place because the building is not just old, it is structurally unusual: the National Trust describes it as London’s last remaining galleried inn. It feels like a surviving piece of coaching-inn London somehow left behind in modern Southwark. [Venue](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/london/george-inn) · [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+George+Inn+77+Borough+High+Street+London)

### 3. The Churchill Arms, Kensington

Yes, everyone photographs the exterior. Fine. They should. But the pub survives because it is more than a floral façade: the venue says it dates to **1750**, and its long-running Thai kitchen is part of what makes it one of the few highly touristed London pubs that still feels genuinely useful. [Venue](https://www.churchillarmskensington.co.uk/) · [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Churchill+Arms+119+Kensington+Church+Street+London)

### 4. The Spaniards Inn, Hampstead

The venue says it was built in **1585**, and the room still carries that old-roadside-inn atmosphere beautifully. It works best after a walk on Hampstead Heath, when the pub feels less like a destination and more like the correct ending to the day. [Venue](https://www.thespaniardshampstead.co.uk/) · [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Spaniards+Inn+Spaniards+Road+Hampstead+London)

### 5. The Blackfriar, Blackfriars

Distinctive enough that even people who are not pub obsessives tend to remember it. The appeal is visual as much as liquid: this is a pub with enough decorative identity to feel like its own minor London category. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Blackfriar+174+Queen+Victoria+Street+London)

### 6. The Princess Louise, Holborn

One of the strongest Victorian pub interiors in central London. Etched glass, dark wood, old partitions and proper compartmentalised pub drama make it feel like a better version of the city’s past rather than a theme-park reconstruction. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Princess+Louise+208+High+Holborn+London)

## Soho and the centre

### 7. The French House, Soho

Small, famous, stubborn, and still unusually itself. The French House remains one of the best Soho answers because it understands compression: a packed room, short pours, fast conversation, and just enough mythology to keep it humming. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+French+House+49+Dean+Street+London)

### 8. The Devonshire, Soho

The newer entry that already feels written into the city. The success of The Devonshire comes from understanding something simple: London still wants pubs that feel pub-like, not over-softened concept spaces pretending to be casual. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Devonshire+17+Denman+Street+London)

### 9. The Harp, Covent Garden

Tiny, busy, and still one of central London’s strongest pints. It earns loyalty because it feels serious without becoming joyless, which is rare in such a dense tourist zone. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Harp+47+Chandos+Place+London)

### 10. The Lamb, Bloomsbury

A proper Bloomsbury pub: literary-adjacent, handsome, and quietly self-assured. You come here for the room as much as the drink, especially if you like your pub with a trace of old editorial London. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Lamb+94+Lambs+Conduit+Street+London)

### 11. The Coach & Horses, Soho

Soho used to be full of pubs with grit, opinion and a certain anti-polish energy. The Coach & Horses still carries some of that residue, which is why it remains worth keeping in the rotation. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Coach+%26+Horses+29+Greek+Street+London)

### 12. The Old Mitre, Holborn

Tucked away enough to still feel slightly secret even though everyone knows about it. The charm here is partly architectural, partly locational: it feels detached from the main drag in exactly the right way. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Old+Mitre+1+Ely+Court+Ely+Place+London)

## North London classics

### 13. The Flask, Highgate

Highgate specialises in making ordinary pub-going feel faintly refined, and The Flask plays that role well. It is compact, old, and useful in all the right ways — the kind of pub that improves grey afternoons. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Flask+77+Highgate+West+Hill+London)

### 14. The Holly Bush, Hampstead

One of London’s best neighbourhood pubs full stop. It has the kind of low-lit, calm, no-show-off comfort that makes people stay far longer than they planned. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Holly+Bush+22+Holly+Mount+London)

### 15. The Southampton Arms, Kentish Town

Beer-first, nonsense-free, and proud of it. This is one of the most convincing arguments for pub culture as a taste system rather than just a drinking venue. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Southampton+Arms+139+Highgate+Road+London)

### 16. The Faltering Fullback, Finsbury Park

A garden pub that actually deserves the reputation. The layered outdoor spaces give it a sociable looseness that turns even a quick stop into a long one. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Faltering+Fullback+19+Perseverance+Tavern+London)

### 17. The Albion, Islington

A very strong Islington answer: large enough to be useful, attractive enough to feel worth dressing for, and balanced enough to work for drinks, lunch or a lazy Sunday. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Albion+10+Thornhill+Road+London)

### 18. The Sekforde, Clerkenwell

Quietly one of the better central-ish pubs when you want a room that does not shout at you. The point here is tone: measured, civilised, and confident enough not to overperform. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Sekforde+34+Sekforde+Street+London)

## Riverside and East London

### 19. The Dove, Hammersmith

The Thames does a lot of work here, but the pub earns the view. Riverside pubs live or die on whether they feel like part of the river rather than just adjacent to it; The Dove passes that test. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Dove+19+Upper+Mall+London)

### 20. The Mayflower, Rotherhithe

Atmospheric in a way that feels earned rather than marketed. Timber, river, low light, and the sense that you are slightly further from the city than you really are. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Mayflower+117+Rotherhithe+Street+London)

### 21. Prospect of Whitby, Wapping

One of London’s classic river pubs and still among the best settings for bad weather and a long pint. Some pubs need sunshine; this one benefits from a little wind and a darker sky. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Prospect+of+Whitby+57+Wapping+Wall+London)

### 22. The Grapes, Limehouse

Small, dark, and just the right side of romantic. The room feels compact in the way good East London riverside pubs should, and the whole place rewards slower drinking. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Grapes+76+Narrow+Street+London)

### 23. The Gun, Docklands

A strong answer for anyone who thinks Docklands has no soul. The water, the room, and the slight feeling of separation from the rest of London all work in its favour. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Gun+27+Cologne+Road+London)

### 24. The Anchor, Bankside

Yes, it gets busy. Still useful. The location near the river and the density of everything around it make it a practical and often rewarding stop in a very walkable part of the city. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Anchor+34+Park+Street+London)

## South and southeast

### 25. The Camberwell Arms, Camberwell

One of the best examples of the modern London gastropub done properly. It still feels like a pub first, which is exactly why the food lands harder. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Camberwell+Arms+65+Camberwell+Church+Street+London)

### 26. The Duke of Edinburgh, Brixton

A large, flexible pub that can absorb groups, dates, roast-seekers and aimless drifters without collapsing into chaos. That adaptability is part of the achievement. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Duke+of+Edinburgh+204+Ferndale+Road+London)

### 27. The Blythe Hill Tavern, Forest Hill

A neighbourhood pub worth leaving your own neighbourhood for. Spacious, handsome, and reliable in the deeply underrated craft of making an ordinary evening feel properly sorted. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Blythe+Hill+Tavern+319+Stanstead+Road+London)

### 28. The Crown & Greyhound, Dulwich

Big enough to feel useful, polished enough to feel occasion-ready, but still comfortable enough to remain a genuine pub. Very strong for a longer lunch or a stretched-out Sunday. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Crown+%26+Greyhound+73+Dulwich+Village+London)

## West London and the polished end

### 29. The Cow, Notting Hill

Still one of west London’s best pub-and-food combinations. The room has enough actual pub energy to stop the meal from becoming too mannered, which is why it continues to work. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Cow+89+Westbourne+Park+Road+London)

### 30. The Guinea Grill, Mayfair

For when the pub and chop-house impulses need to meet. The appeal is not trendiness but old-world assurance: a room that still understands Mayfair before everything became softer, shinier, and less convincing. [Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The+Guinea+Grill+30+Bruton+Place+London)

## Final pint

A London pub list is never really finished because the city does not use pubs in one way.

Some are for history. Some are for Guinness. Some are for recovering from central London. Some are for dates, rain, roast dinners, football, grief, deadlines, gossip, first meetings, and final rounds.

What matters is not whether a pub is famous. It is whether it still does the thing properly.
