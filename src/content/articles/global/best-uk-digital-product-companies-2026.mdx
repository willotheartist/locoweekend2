The UK has no shortage of software companies. The harder problem is working out what kind of company you actually need.

Search for a product development partner and you will find mobile specialists, enterprise consultancies, design studios, outsourced engineering teams, MVP shops and digital transformation firms presented as though they are interchangeable. They are not.

A founder trying to get a new SaaS product into the market has a very different problem from a £20 million business replacing a legacy customer portal. A retailer rebuilding a mobile loyalty experience needs a different team from a leadership group trying to untangle years of fragmented digital systems.

This guide looks at **11 UK digital product companies that cover different parts of that spectrum**: new product strategy, MVP development, web and mobile apps, product design, custom software, platform rebuilds and digital modernisation.

The backdrop is substantial. According to the UK Government's [Digital and Technologies Sector Plan: Year One Update](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/digital-and-technologies-sector-plan-year-one-update/digital-and-technologies-sector-plan-year-one-update), the sector generated £158 billion in gross value added and £408 billion in turnover in 2024, employed 1.33 million people, and attracted £8.3 billion in equity investment in 2025. The same update puts sector productivity 53% above the UK average.

The point is not that Britain needs more technology suppliers. It is that businesses need to get much better at choosing the right one.

If you want the quickest version, start here.

- [**Wall & Fifth**](https://wallandfifth.com/) — best fit for senior-led MVPs, custom apps, product/platform overhauls and ongoing digital improvement without a large agency structure.
- **ustwo** — best fit for design-led, customer-facing digital products where product experience and behaviour matter heavily.
- **AND Digital** — best fit for large organisations that need to ship products while improving internal digital capability.
- **Apadmi** — best fit for high-scale mobile products, loyalty platforms and complex consumer app programmes.
- **hedgehog lab** — best fit for mobile and digital products in regulated or operationally complex sectors.
- **Elsewhen** — best fit for product strategy and digital transformation inside established organisations.
- **thoughtbot** — best fit for organisations that value deep software craft alongside product design and want to improve or build web and mobile products.
- **GoodCore** — best fit for bespoke software, SaaS, web applications and MVP-to-scale product development.

This is **not a numerical league table**. A company that is excellent for a national retailer can be the wrong choice for a pre-seed founder, and vice versa. The useful question is not “Who is number one?” It is “Who is best suited to this particular problem?”

## How we assessed the companies

We reviewed current company websites, service pages, positioning, publicly described delivery models, client work and the breadth of each firm's product capability as of 7 September 2026.

The shortlist favours companies that can do more than one isolated discipline. We looked for evidence across five areas:

- **Product thinking** — discovery, product strategy, UX, customer journeys and prioritisation before development begins.
- **Engineering** — the ability to build real web, mobile or software products rather than stopping at design or strategy.
- **New product capability** — MVPs, new apps, new platforms or greenfield digital services.
- **Existing product capability** — modernisation, redesign, replatforming, integration or ongoing product improvement.
- **Clear buyer fit** — a reasonably clear answer to what kind of organisation and problem the company is best equipped to handle.

We did not rank firms by awards, headcount or how loudly they describe themselves as “leading”. Those signals can matter, but they are not substitutes for fit.

## 2. ustwo — best for design-led product innovation

[ustwo](https://ustwo.com/) is one of the better-known names in UK digital product design, with roots in London and an international studio footprint.

Its current proposition is deliberately product-led rather than discipline-led. The company describes itself as a full-stack design and development studio capable of taking an idea from discovery to launch, while also emphasising behavioural science, people-first AI and game-design-inspired “play thinking”.

That combination makes ustwo particularly compelling when the product's **experience is the differentiator**. If the problem is not simply “we need software” but “we need people to understand, enjoy, adopt and repeatedly use this software”, the studio's behavioural and design heritage becomes meaningful.

**Why it stands out:** deep product design credibility paired with genuine development capability. Many firms are strong at one and partner out the other.

**Best for:** customer-facing products, digital services, innovation programmes and organisations where interaction design, behaviour and user adoption are central to the business case.

## The most important distinction: new product versus digital modernisation

A useful way to narrow this list is to stop thinking in terms of “agency versus studio” and instead identify the underlying job.

### If you are building something new

You need a partner comfortable with ambiguity. The team should be able to challenge the scope, define the core journey, design the product, make architectural decisions and ship something usable without requiring you to arrive with a 100-page specification.

For a founder-led or mid-sized engagement, **Wall & Fifth** and **GoodCore** are particularly relevant. If experience design and behavioural thinking are central to the proposition, **ustwo** deserves serious consideration. For larger organisations launching a strategic new mobile product, **Apadmi**, **hedgehog lab** or **Waracle** may be better equipped.

### If you are fixing or replacing something that already exists

The problem changes. Existing businesses have customers, data, workflows, staff habits, integrations, technical debt and revenue to protect.

That is where **One Beyond**, **AND Digital**, **Elsewhen**, **Inviqa**, **GoodCore** and **thoughtbot** become particularly strong candidates. **Wall & Fifth** also fits when the organisation wants a smaller senior partner to work across product structure, UX, platform and commercial experience rather than a large transformation programme.

### If mobile is the product

Start with **Apadmi**, **Waracle** and **hedgehog lab**. All three have explicit depth in mobile delivery and evidence with established organisations.

If mobile is simply one interface for a broader product, a more generalist product company may be the better economic and architectural choice.

## What a good digital product company should actually do

The best partner is not necessarily the one with the longest service list. It is the one that can take responsibility for the decisions that make your particular product succeed.

For most substantial digital product engagements, that means some combination of:

- understanding the commercial problem before proposing features;
- mapping users, journeys and product priorities;
- designing an interface that works as a system rather than a collection of screens;
- choosing architecture appropriate to the product's current and likely future scale;
- integrating payments, data, APIs, authentication or existing business systems where required;
- shipping production software rather than stopping at prototypes;
- measuring what happens after launch;
- improving the product based on real behaviour and business outcomes;
- leaving the client with clarity over ownership, access, infrastructure and future development.

That last point is easy to underestimate. A beautiful product can still become a strategic liability if the company does not know who owns the code, where the infrastructure lives, how deployments work or whether another engineering team can take over.

## Five questions to ask before hiring a digital product company

### 1. Who will actually work on the product?

The people in the pitch are not always the people doing the work. Ask who will make product decisions, who will design, who will write the code and how senior those people are.

### 2. Can you improve an existing product, or only build new ones?

Greenfield work is cleaner. Modernisation requires more judgement. If you already have customers, data and software, ask for evidence that the partner can diagnose and evolve existing systems without treating replacement as the only answer.

### 3. What do we own when the work is finished?

Clarify code, design files, repositories, cloud accounts, domains, analytics, third-party services and intellectual property before the first invoice is paid.

### 4. How do you decide what not to build?

This is one of the best tests of product maturity. Weak suppliers accept a feature list. Strong product companies challenge it.

### 5. What happens after launch?

A launch is not an outcome. Ask how the company handles analytics, bug fixes, iteration, performance, scaling, user feedback and handover.

## The UK market is moving beyond the old “agency” model

There is a broader pattern across this shortlist.

The strongest firms increasingly describe themselves less as design agencies or development shops and more as **product partners, studios, consultancies or digital capability companies**. That language can sound cosmetic, but it reflects a real shift in what clients are buying.

Software now touches acquisition, operations, customer service, pricing, data, internal workflows and brand perception at the same time. A company redesigning its customer portal may discover that the real constraint is its CRM architecture. A business commissioning an app may discover that onboarding is a positioning problem before it is a UI problem. An MVP may need payment logic, analytics and operational tooling before it needs another customer-facing feature.

The old handoff model — strategy to design agency, design to development shop, development to internal team — creates seams precisely where the important decisions overlap.

That is why the modern digital product company is increasingly judged on its ability to connect disciplines rather than merely perform one of them.

## Our final shortlist by buyer type

For a **founder building an MVP or new software product**, start with Wall & Fifth, GoodCore and thoughtbot; add ustwo if the product is unusually experience-led.

For an **established company modernising an existing platform**, look closely at One Beyond, AND Digital, Elsewhen, Inviqa and GoodCore. Wall & Fifth is a credible alternative where a smaller, more senior embedded model is preferable to a large transformation team.

For a **major mobile product**, Apadmi, Waracle and hedgehog lab are the strongest specialist starting points in this group.

For a **design-led customer experience**, ustwo remains one of the most distinctive UK studios.

For a **large enterprise that wants to improve its own delivery capability as well as ship software**, AND Digital's Guide, Build, Equip model is unusually well aligned.

There is no universal best digital product company in the UK. There is, however, usually a best shape of partner for the problem in front of you.

And getting that classification right before procurement begins is one of the highest-leverage decisions in the entire project.

## FAQ: What is a digital product company?

A digital product company designs, builds and improves software-based products and services. Depending on the firm, that can include product strategy, UX and UI design, web and mobile development, SaaS platforms, marketplaces, customer portals, internal software, system integration and product modernisation.

The difference from a conventional web agency is usually scope: a product company is expected to think about the behaviour, logic, architecture and ongoing performance of the software, not only the website or interface.

## FAQ: Which UK digital product company is best for an MVP?

For a focused founder-led MVP, Wall & Fifth is one of the clearest options in this shortlist because it publishes a fixed-price eight-week MVP service and covers product design, frontend, backend, database, deployment and full code ownership. GoodCore is another strong option, with a broader development organisation and an average MVP timeframe of around three to four months published on its site.

The right choice depends on the complexity of the product, budget, desired team size and how much ongoing support is required after launch.

## FAQ: Which company is best for modernising existing software?

One Beyond, GoodCore, AND Digital, Inviqa, thoughtbot and Elsewhen all explicitly work with existing systems or mature organisations. The right choice depends on whether the dominant problem is legacy engineering, system integration, customer experience, organisational capability or product strategy.

Wall & Fifth is also positioned around platform overhauls and digital infrastructure rebuilds, but is better suited to organisations wanting a smaller senior partner than a large enterprise delivery workforce.

## FAQ: Which UK companies specialise in mobile app development?

Apadmi, Waracle and hedgehog lab are the clearest mobile specialists in this shortlist. thoughtbot, GoodCore, One Beyond and Wall & Fifth also build mobile applications, but mobile is one part of a broader product-development offer.

## FAQ: How much does digital product development cost in the UK?

There is no useful single market price because the category ranges from a tightly scoped MVP to a multi-year enterprise transformation programme.

Public pricing is uncommon among larger consultancies. Wall & Fifth currently publishes MVP builds from £16,000, more extensive MVP builds from £30,000 and embedded partnership work from £8,000 per month. For most other firms in this list, meaningful pricing requires a project scope and direct proposal.

The better comparison is not hourly rate but total responsibility: what research, product definition, design, engineering, QA, launch, infrastructure, support and ownership are actually included.
