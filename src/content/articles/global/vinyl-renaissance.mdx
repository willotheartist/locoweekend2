For years it looked like the record shop would disappear entirely.

Streaming platforms replaced physical music collections almost overnight. Entire high-street music chains collapsed.

Yet something unexpected happened.

Vinyl records came back.

## The Return of Physical Music

Vinyl sales have been rising steadily for more than a decade. In several countries, vinyl now outsells CDs.

But the real story is not just the format. It is the cultural ecosystem that surrounds it.

Independent record shops are returning to cities across Europe.

## Why People Still Love Record Shops

Record shops offer something streaming platforms cannot: discovery.

Walking through shelves of albums, talking to knowledgeable staff, and attending in-store performances creates a social experience around music.

It turns listening into an event.

## London's New Record Culture

In London, independent stores like Rough Trade and smaller neighbourhood shops continue to attract new audiences.

Many of their customers are younger listeners who never experienced vinyl the first time around.

For them, records feel new.

## Music As A Physical Culture

Streaming made music frictionless.

But vinyl restores ritual: choosing a record, placing the needle, listening to an album from start to finish.

In an era of endless playlists, that slower experience has become unexpectedly appealing.

The vinyl revival proves that technology rarely erases culture completely.

Sometimes it simply changes its form.
