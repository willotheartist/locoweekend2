For a night out in Madrid, start with **La Santoria, Fun Fun, Angelita, La Venencia, 1862 Dry Bar, Salmon Guru or La Vía Láctea**. ## 1. La Santoria: cocktails with a side of divine intervention

**Go for:** A theatrical setting and a cocktail conversation that ventures beyond your preferred spirit.  
**Find it:** Calle de Lope de Vega, 30, Barrio de las Letras.

There are bars where you order a martini, and there are bars where the menu appears to have opinions about your spiritual condition.

La Santoria belongs to the second category. Saints, rosaries, flowers and candlelight form the backdrop to drinks presented as potions and elixirs. Falstaff describes an approach in which the conversation can begin with your mood and desires, rather than a straightforward list of ingredients. Think of the supernatural language as part of the performance; the practical business remains choosing something you will enjoy drinking. [Falstaff's La Santoria profile](https://www.falstaff.com/en/bars/la-santoria).

That makes it an appealing choice for an evening that needs a little momentum. The room gives you something to talk about before you have exhausted the usual questions about work.

**What to order:** Explain whether you like something dry, bitter, fruity or spirit-forward, then explore the house selection. A drink's mystical job description is entertaining, but your feelings about sweetness remain relevant.

**Know before you go:** The venue's own menu page says it does **not take reservations**. It serves cocktails with and without alcohol, plus small plates. Follow that information over third-party listings suggesting you book. [Official menu](https://lasantoria.com/cocktailbarmenu).

## 2. Fun Fun: give the wine a little attention

**Go for:** Natural wine and an evening organised around conversation.  
**Find it:** Calle de Don Pedro, 20, La Latina; address reported by El País.

Fun Fun brings a different pace to this selection. El País's 2024 reporting describes its natural-wine focus and changing selections. Its reported kimchi-and-orange-wine pairing offers a glimpse of the approach, although it should not be treated as a guaranteed current menu item. [El País's report](https://elpais.com/espana/madrid/2024-10-25/la-exposicion-el-fin-del-amor-un-club-del-vino-natural-y-una-clase-de-sushi-tres-planazos-en-madrid-para-resignificar-los-espacios.html).

**What to order:** Ask what is open, describe what you enjoy and give a price range. There is no entrance exam for being interested in wine. “Fresh, dry and around this much” is a perfectly respectable opening sentence.

**Know before you go:** Check the current offering through [Fun Fun's official site](https://funfunmadrid.com/). For a wider wine-led shortlist, see our guide to [wine bars in Madrid](/articles/best-wine-bars-in-madrid-right-now).

## 3. Angelita: follow the cocktail back to the allotment

**Go for:** An ingredient story with actual people and a place behind it.  
**Find it:** Calle de la Reina, 4, near Gran Vía.

“Seasonal” becomes more interesting when someone can tell you where the season happened.

At Angelita, Madrid's tourism office traces some cocktail ingredients to the Villalón family's allotment in Zamora. Brothers David and Mario Villalón run an operation encompassing wine, dining and a basement cocktail bar. That connection between growing, cooking and drinking gives the place a stronger premise than a menu merely decorated with botanical names. [Madrid tourism's Angelita profile](https://www.esmadrid.com/en/nightlife/angelita-madrid).

The question to ask here is what the team is working with now. It opens a more revealing conversation than requesting whichever drink looks most dramatic online.

**What to order:** Ask for a cocktail that expresses the current ingredient selection, while being clear about flavours you dislike. Curiosity works better when it comes with a little direction.

**Know before you go:** Angelita has different spaces and experiences. Specify whether you want the restaurant, wine bar or cocktail bar when checking arrangements. A dinner reservation and a plan for a basement drink are different things.

## 4. La Venencia: sherry deserves an evening of its own

**Go for:** Jerez wines, salty accompaniments and the pleasure of a focused tavern.  
**Find it:** Calle de Echegaray, 7, Barrio de las Letras.

An enormous drinks list can create the peculiar experience of having unlimited choice and no idea what you want. La Venencia provides a more defined brief.

The tavern specialises in Jerez wines and manzanilla, with accompaniments including mojama—dried tuna—anchovies, olives and pickles. Dark wood and barrels complete a setting built around a particular way of drinking. [Madrid tourism's La Venencia profile](https://www.esmadrid.com/en/restaurants/venencia).

It earns its place because a Madrid bar guide should make room for this kind of specificity. Sherry can be the reason to choose an address, rather than something discovered at the forgotten end of a wine list.

**What to order:** Ask about a manzanilla and something salty to eat. If sherry is unfamiliar territory, say so. A useful first glass is more valuable than pretending to recognise every name.

**Know before you go:** Choose La Venencia because you want to explore its speciality. Arriving with a fixed demand for an elaborate cocktail rather misses the opportunity.

## 5. 1862 Dry Bar: the date above the door is a clue

**Go for:** Cocktail history and a more restrained setting.  
**Find it:** Calle del Pez, 27, Malasaña.

The name refers to the publication year of Jerry Thomas's cocktail book. Before you have opened a menu, 1862 Dry Bar has already declared an interest.

The 50's profile identifies owner Alberto Martínez, an extensive spirits collection and an intimate downstairs area. Its description places the drinks at the centre of the operation, with relatively understated surroundings. [The 50's 1862 Dry Bar profile](https://www.the50.com/discovery/Establishments/Spain/Madrid/1862-Dry-Bar.html).

That makes it a useful address for the person in your group who wants to discuss what is in the glass. There is room in an evening for that particular enthusiasm. You may even discover you share it.

**What to order:** Use a familiar favourite as a starting point, then ask what the bar would suggest in that direction. “I usually like a Manhattan” gives the conversation somewhere concrete to begin.

**Know before you go:** The historical reference is part of the bar's identity, not evidence that the business opened in 1862. It is a small distinction, but the kind that gets lost remarkably quickly in travel writing.

## 6. Salmon Guru: let the drinks get a little strange

**Go for:** Visual excess and combinations that invite questions.  
**Find it:** Calle de Echegaray, 21, Barrio de las Letras.

Salmon Guru makes a strong case for occasionally abandoning restraint.

The 50's account of its 2023 redesign describes neon and comic-book murals, alongside drinks whose ingredients have included fish sauce, fermented strawberry, balsamic vinegar and Pepsi. The detail matters because it establishes a willingness to play with flavours as well as appearances. [The 50's Salmon Guru profile](https://www.the50.com/discovery/Establishments/Spain/Madrid/Salmon-Guru.html).

A surprising ingredient is a good opening for a conversation with the bartender. What is it doing in the drink? Does it add acidity, savouriness or something else? The answer is potentially more interesting than another photograph of the glass.

**What to order:** Pick something from the current menu that intrigues you and ask for an explanation before committing. Curiosity does not oblige you to enjoy every experiment.

**Know before you go:** Specific drinks in published profiles can belong to earlier menus. Check the [official venue website](https://www.salmonguru.es/) for current information. For more cocktail-focused reading, continue with our [Madrid cocktail-bar guide](/articles/best-cocktail-bars-in-madrid-right-now).

## 7. La Vía Láctea: music history with somewhere to put your drink

**Go for:** A connection to the Movida madrileña and a music-led change of pace.  
**Find it:** Calle de Velarde, 18, Malasaña.

A former coal yard is a promising place to begin a nightlife story.

La Vía Láctea opened in 1979 and became associated with the Movida madrileña. Madrid's tourism office documents its links with musicians, DJs and artists, and the city recognised that history with a plaque in 2021. The venue occupies two floors. [Madrid tourism's La Vía Láctea profile](https://www.esmadrid.com/en/nightlife/la-via-lactea).

The history provides context, though it cannot tell you exactly what a particular night will feel like. That part still belongs to the people and music in the room.

It is a useful addition to this list because an evening eventually needs to become more than a discussion of what everyone ordered.

**What to order:** Choose a straightforward drink you already enjoy and direct your attention towards the music.

**Know before you go:** Check the venue's current information if you are going for a particular event. Its historical associations are a reason for curiosity, rather than a promise of a scheduled live performance.

## Choose a neighbourhood, then leave some room in the plan

Seven addresses are a shortlist for several evenings. Trying to complete them all would turn a useful guide into administrative work. If you are still choosing a base, our guide to [Madrid neighbourhoods for a weekend](/articles/best-neighbourhoods-in-madrid-for-a-weekend) helps put the evening in the context of the rest of your stay.

For **Barrio de las Letras**, choose between La Venencia, La Santoria and Salmon Guru according to the mood you want. La Venencia and Salmon Guru are on the same street, but their premises could hardly be more different: one gives you a focused introduction to sherry; the other offers elaborate contemporary cocktails. La Santoria adds its own theatrical world on nearby Lope de Vega.

For **Malasaña**, 1862 Dry Bar and La Vía Láctea offer two different priorities within the neighbourhood. Start by deciding whether you want the drink or the music to lead. There is no obligation to move on if the first place suits the evening.

Choose **Fun Fun** when wine is the main interest. Choose **Angelita** when you want to investigate the relationship between ingredients, food and cocktails. Either can be the destination around which you organise everything else.

Before heading out, check the venue's current opening information and booking policy. Set a budget before asking for recommendations, especially when exploring unfamiliar bottles or spirits. Specific preferences are helpful; pretending you have none rarely improves the order.

For the following morning, save our [Madrid cafés guide](/articles/best-cafes-in-madrid-for-reading-writing-and-hiding-out). Then put the shortlist away. The point of choosing a good bar is to spend some time in it.
